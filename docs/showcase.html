A queue, on a desk, in slow motion.

Three runs of the library, recorded from the examples that ship inside the module. Press play and the transcript types itself out; the lines are the ones the example printed, not a description of them.

One task, one retry

An app over the in-memory broker, one worker, two tasks: one that answers, and one that fails once and is tried again after a second of backoff. The clock belongs to the example, which is why the ids read the same every time.


  

  

    
    
  


  
Three cards on one string

  
A chain runs its links in order and answers the last; a group runs together and answers a list; a chord counts its parts and sends one callback with all their results.


  

    

    

  

  

    
    
  


  
One run per item, per tuple, per slice

  
A map sends one run per item; a starmap sends one per tuple, the tuple spread across that run's arguments; a chunk sends one message per slice of tuples and runs the slice in the worker that took it. Each answers the results in the order the items were given, and a chunk answers one list per slice.


  

    

    

  

  

    
    
  


  
Why a transcript rather than a sandbox

  
A queue is a thing that waits: its guarantees are about what happens after a worker dies, not about what happens in a browser tab. The recordings above are the honest shape of it — a run of the real code, on a clock the example controls, so the same lines come out every time and the workflow faults when they stop matching.
